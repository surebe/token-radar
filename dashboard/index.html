How a row gets its number

Three steps, all fitted by the program on the snapshot itself — no hand-kept list, no carried-in coefficients. Conditional value: what the token would be worth if it launched. Launch odds: how likely a launch is, read off the population. Implied value = conditional value × launch odds, the ranking.

The slope is the finding. A flat TVL × multiple rule assumes value grows in proportion to TVL (slope 1); the traded population says 0.52. At $10M of TVL the two rules roughly agree; at $1B the flat rule prices a launch about five times higher than the fitted line, and at $10B about fifteen times. The giants are exactly where a radar earns its keep, and exactly where the flat rule is most wrong. Averages are worse than either: the mean recorded multiple is poisoned by outliers (one protocol trades near 400× its TVL), so every "typical" figure here is a median or a geometric fit.

Launch odds

Read the odds as prevalence, not prophecy: the fit learns how often protocols with these figures already record a token, which mixes "will launch" with "already did". Brier scores punish confident wrongness; lower is better, and the base-rate row is the score for guessing the population average for everyone.

Range honesty

Fancier variants were fitted and rejected on the evidence: adding growth, age or category terms to the value fit, and a saturating (logistic-in-TVL) form, each win on some time splits and lose on others, so none earns its complexity. The simple sub-linear line is the model because it is the one that holds up out-of-sample.

Farm economics

Each category farms by an archetype — the action, a season's transaction count, setup and upkeep minutes, a sensible minimum capital. A row's verdict is its plan's net dollars per hour of farmer time:

pool = conditional value × drop share  ·  intensity = pool ÷ TVL  ·  net $/h = (intensity × capital − gas − 5%/yr × capital × weeks/52) ÷ hours

Assumptions, stated once and used everywhere: drop share 10% of launch value reaches farmers; season 16 weeks; opportunity cost 5%/yr on locked capital; gas per transaction priced on the cheapest chain the protocol runs on — Ethereum $8, major L2s $0.15, Solana $0.02, anything else $0.50. Verdicts: FARM at $25/h or better, MARGINAL $5–25/h, SKIP below that or where no retail path exists. One consequence worth knowing: when a row's intensity is under the season's opportunity-cost floor (about 1.54% at 16 weeks), no position size breaks even — the drop share itself would have to be larger. Each detail view prints its row's break-even capital, or says there is none.

What the data cannot say

"Records no token" is the snapshot's wording made precise: both its token fields (CoinGecko id and symbol) are empty. A protocol can carry one field without the other — 258 do — so a one-field rule misreads the population in both directions. Listing dates are missing for 113 protocols, two of them on the watchlist; their ages are imputed at the population median and flagged on their rows. The value fit learns from the 128 traded tokens that record a market cap; the other 272 launched protocols record none, so the fit describes protocols that report a cap, not all that trade. Seven-day growth is winsorized to [−50%, +100%] because the raw field runs to +4,448%.
